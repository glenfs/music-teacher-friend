MusicEd / Clefira — Teacher Experience Roadmap

What it takes to make the app awesome and great for music teachers.
Strategic analysis · 2026-05-20 · Source: codebase walk-through (Practice Drills, Teacher Dashboard, Learning Mode, generator system, per-student data layer)

Big-picture framing

Right now MusicEd is a great practice app with a teacher dashboard bolted on. The most valuable version of it is something different: a piano teacher's studio in software, where the practice app is the most-used surface but only one part of the system.

Teachers don't just need more drills; they need their job easier — student visibility, parent communication, prep time, recital coordination, billing. The good news: you already have most of the bones (per-student data routing, lesson sessions, dashboard with five tabs, procedural exercise generation, learning curriculum). The path to "awesome" is mostly filling in the workflow layer that turns the existing practice content into a studio system.

What's already strong (the foundation)

  • Per-student data architecture — ActiveStudent, per-student user://students/<id>/ layout, lesson sessions. Table-stakes that competitors often fail at.
  • 8-mode practice depth — Interval, Chord, Sight Notes/Chords/Grand Staff, Rhythm Flow, Note Chase, Progression, Scale/Mode, Cadence. Few apps have all of these.
  • 22-module Learning Mode — structured curriculum with stars, gating, review queue.
  • Procedural exercise generation — Phases 1–8 of the generator system. Almost no competitor offers this; it is genuinely a moat.
  • Teacher Dashboard with Overview / Repertoire / Lesson Log / Assignments / Technique — most apps have one or two of these tabs, not all five.
  • Practice Drills with MIDI keyboard support + scripts/audio/pitch_detector.gd (currently underused).
  • End-of-lesson dialog that prompts a lesson log entry (Phase 3 work).

That's a strong base. Build on it.

Tier 1 — The items that move "good" to "great"

01 Critical Cloud sync

Already on the roadmap as item #8. The single most blocking gap.

A piano teacher uses a tablet in the studio and a laptop at home; without sync, the dashboard is fragmented. Everything else (favorites, parent portal, multi-teacher) depends on a sync layer. Bring this forward.

02 Critical Practice journal — the "between-lessons" view

The single highest-ROI feature for teacher experience.

When a teacher opens the dashboard at lesson start, they should see at a glance: "Sarah practiced 4 days since last lesson (28 min total), completed Hanon 3 + 4, accuracy on m6 intervals jumped from 42% → 71%."

The data is already being collected (session_history, per-student progress files); what's missing is a timeline view on the Overview tab — a sparkline of daily practice minutes + a feed of what happened. Turns the app into a window into the student's home life with the instrument.

Where: Extend the Overview tab in teacher_dashboard.gd; aggregate from per-student progress JSON.
03 Critical Personalized weakness-driven drills

Phase 9 of the generator plan — already deferred but high impact.

The review queue tracks which intervals/notes/chords a student misses. Pipe that into hanon_any_random / czerny_random / a new surprise_me entry so each generated exercise targets what this student needs. Teachers will instantly notice this — "the app actually knows my student."

Pair with ★ Save Favorite (the 6-field tuple (generator_id, key, level, hand, octaves, seed) already plumbed through).

04 Critical Auto-generated parent progress reports

Parents pay tuition; they want to see results.

Once a week (or end-of-lesson), generate a clean PDF/HTML for parents: cover image, child's name, practice minutes, accuracy progression, what they're working on next. You already have _save_printable_lesson_summary writing HTML; extend it to a recurring weekly format with a shareable link.

This is the feature that gets parents to keep paying — and tells them what they're paying for.

05 Critical Lesson plan templates + "Today's Agenda" card

Most teachers reuse lesson patterns — opening warm-up, review pieces, new piece, ear training, assignments.

Build:

  • A Lesson Plan templating area (dashboard sidebar) where teachers compose: [Scale in 3 keys @120bpm] → [Hanon 1 in Cmaj] → [Ear training: minor 3rds] → [Sight reading: 10 bars]
  • A "Today's Agenda" card on Overview — loads the assigned template or auto-generates from the student's level + recent activity.
  • One-tap "start each step" buttons that launch the right mode preconfigured.

This is what makes a teacher feel the app helps them teach, not just helps the student drill.

06 Critical Recital / performance prep mode

Recital prep is the most stressful part of a teacher's year — own this and you own teachers.

Mark pieces as "for recital" with a date. Track BPM progression toward target. Run-through mode plays the full piece while the student plays along, recording via mic. Compare passes side-by-side over weeks.

07 Critical Mic-based recording + playback

Activates an unused asset already in the codebase.

You have pitch_detector.gd sitting in scripts/audio/. Tap into the mic for:

  • Sight-singing — show a melody on the staff, student sings it, app detects pitch and rates accuracy. There is no good sight-singing app on tablets right now.
  • Practice recordings — student records attempts; teacher reviews between lessons; both can hear progression over time.
  • "Was that in tune?" — instant feedback on any single note or interval.
08 Critical Multi-teacher mode

Item #7 deferred. Studios with subs / multiple instructors need this.

A studio might have a primary teacher plus a sub. "Mrs. Johnson is subbing today; she sees the same student dashboards plus my prep notes." Add a Teachers entity sibling to Students, reassignable per session.

09 Critical Per-student daily streak

Item #4 deferred. Quick win given per-student folders are in place.

Currently device-wide. With per-student data routing already there, this is a small change for outsized engagement payoff. Streak Freeze (let a student skip one day without losing their streak) is a small gamification that meaningfully raises retention.

10 Critical Group class mode

An increasing share of teachers run small group lessons (4–8 kids).

2–4 students share one iPad and take turns, with a leaderboard; or each student on their own device joins a "studio session" the teacher controls. Even basic team scoring on a scale race lets group teachers replace whiteboard games.

Tier 2 — Big differentiators

11 Differentiator AI lesson plan generator

Plug an LLM API into a dashboard button: "Generate a 30-minute lesson for an intermediate student working on F# minor scales and Burgmuller op. 100 No. 5." Outputs a structured agenda the teacher can edit. Real time-saver and a major marketing hook.

12 Differentiator AI recording feedback

Student records a practice attempt; AI flags "timing wavers in mm. 4–6", "left hand softer than right", "you sped up at the cadence". Tonara/Yousician's offerings here are weak — there's an opportunity to be sharper. Pair with the recording feature (#7).

13 Differentiator Detailed analytics dashboard with heatmaps

A 12×8 grid of interval × key colored by accuracy. A timeline of which modules took longest. A forgetting-curve view showing where this student needs review. Teachers love data when it's presented as actionable.

14 Differentiator Calendar / scheduling integration

Recurring lesson slots, reschedule UI, parent reminders via SMS or email. Even a simple iCal export is a meaningful upgrade.

15 Differentiator Billing / invoicing

Track lesson minutes from lesson sessions (you already record start/end). Generate monthly invoices, mark paid/unpaid. Most piano teachers do this in Excel; pulling it into the app is a massive switching cost in your favor.

16 Differentiator Parent portal (read-only)

A separate, password-shared view where parents see their child's practice log, recent recordings, teacher notes, next assignment. Mom doesn't need to install Teacher Edition — she just opens a link.

17 Differentiator Tablet UX polish

Two-page sheet music spread, pinch zoom, Apple Pencil annotations on the staff. Most piano teaching happens on an iPad now — this is your most important physical surface.

18 Differentiator Achievement system with real depth

"Maestro of Major Keys" — completed all 12 majors at 90%+. "Ear of Mozart" — 50-streak on advanced intervals. "Chord Sage" — 1000 chord IDs. Visible on home + dashboard. The current streak is too one-dimensional.

Tier 3 — Depth & content moats

19 Depth Theory beyond Learning Mode

Voice leading drills, Roman numeral analysis, melodic/rhythmic dictation, modulation exercises. The current Learning Mode caps out at the level a serious 4th-year student outgrows.

20 Depth Sheet music engraving polish

The D♭/E♭ bug we just fixed reveals the renderer is still rough. Add: proper beaming for 16ths and triplets, multi-voice notation, dynamics + articulations + slurs, ties spanning bar lines. The Practice Drills view feels homemade until this is polished.

21 Depth More technical-exercise traditions

Bartók Mikrokosmos, Burgmuller op. 100, Kabalevsky Op. 39, Beyer Op. 101. Each is public domain and well-defined — add as new composers alongside Hanon/Czerny/Scale. Becomes a real "technical library".

22 Depth Repertoire library with piece database

Search by composer/level. Mark pages, fingerings, problem spots per student. IMSLP integration to pull actual scores.

23 Depth Multiple piano timbres + ensemble context

Bösendorfer, Steinway, Rhodes, Harpsichord, plus a string-quartet "context" for ear training (intervals in a more musical setting, not just a piano in isolation).

24 Depth Customizable difficulty curves

Teacher overrides the level → tempo mapping for younger students. Save curve as a studio default. One-size-fits-all is the silent enemy of pedagogical apps.

Cross-cutting concerns

25 Cross-cutting Localization

Item #9 deferred. All UI strings are hardcoded English. Blocks non-English markets — and music is a global market. Start with Spanish + Mandarin + French.

26 Cross-cutting Accessibility

Larger-font mode for older teachers (vision matters). Color-blind palette for the heatmap views you'll build. Screen reader support for any text content.

27 Cross-cutting Offline-first

Many teachers work in school basements with weak WiFi. Cloud sync should be last-write-wins with conflict resolution, not strict online-only.

28 Cross-cutting First-run teacher onboarding

A guided tour: add your first student → explore the dashboard → start your first lesson. Include a pre-populated sample student with realistic 3-month history so teachers can see what the dashboard looks like with real data, not an empty shell.

29 Cross-cutting Studio-wide reports + benchmarks

"Your studio's average week-1 streak: 3.2 days." "75% of your students completed Hanon 1 by lesson 4." Useful for teachers thinking about their own pedagogy.

30 Cross-cutting In-app teacher community

Share lesson plan templates and custom exercise recipes with other teachers in the network. Long-tail growth play — your power users become content creators.

★ My Top 3 picks (if budget were finite)

If running this product over the next quarter with limited resources, here's where the leverage is.
★ 1 Top Pick Cloud sync + Practice journal

Combined, these turn the app from a single-device drill tool into a teacher's daily window into every student's progress. Everything else compounds off these two. Cloud sync is already on the roadmap; the journal is small additional work given the data is already collected.

★ 2 Top Pick Auto parent reports + recording/playback

Parents are who pay. Right now parents have zero visibility. A weekly auto-generated PDF report + a "listen to Sarah's latest practice attempt" link will dramatically raise teacher retention by raising parent satisfaction. Teachers love being able to point to evidence when a parent asks "is my kid actually learning?"

★ 3 Top Pick Lesson plan templates + AI-generated agenda

This is the feature that makes a teacher feel the app is helping them teach, not just being a drill machine for students. Most apps stop at "drills for kids"; this jumps you into "studio software for adults". The AI angle is a strong marketing wedge — you're "the music teaching app with AI lesson planning".

Everything else is a multiplier on those three.

Specific code-level observations

  • The 23k-line interval_birds.gd monolith is becoming a liability. Before adding much more, a refactor pass extracting clean modules (the way scripts/learning/, scripts/exercises/, scripts/teacher/ already are) would pay back hugely. Candidates: ear-training session loop, sight-reading state machine, HUD/result-screen UI.
  • The QA bot has 5 known intermittent failures — these are the kind of thing that erode confidence over time. Worth a targeted session.
  • The Learning Mode's "Practice Weak Notes" card is the obvious hook for Phase-9 personalization — that infrastructure exists.
  • The single-color-per-mode design (orange Practice Drills, teal Teacher Dashboard, etc.) is consistent and warm — a real strength worth defending against UI drift.
