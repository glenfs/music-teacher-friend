  Interval
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               1. Chord buttons don't reflect the active pool
  The interval choices are random distractors from whatever you've enabled in settings. If you've only unlocked P4/P5/P8 (beginner), you might still see "TT" or "m2" as choices that are never going to be the answer. Every wrong button
  should be a plausible answer — only show choices from the active pool.

  2. No spaced repetition
  Cadence mode uses ReviewQueue so missed cadences come back more often. Interval and Chord modes don't — they're just random. A player who consistently misses m6 will never get more m6 questions. This is the single biggest learning gap.

  3. Root note range is too narrow and fixed
  Root MIDI is always 52–64 (roughly E3–E4). Real ear training requires hearing intervals from multiple starting pitches. A P5 from C sounds different from a P5 from F# to beginners. Easy sessions should keep a narrow range; harder
  sessions should widen it (e.g. 45–72).

  4. Label abbreviations are cryptic for beginners
  m2, TT, M7 mean nothing to someone just starting. Buttons should show the full name: Minor 2nd, Tritone, Major 7th. The abbreviation can be secondary.

  5. No difficulty progression within a session
  Q1 gets an easy interval (P5), but Q2–Q10 are all equally random. The session should ramp: first 3 questions stay in a comfort zone, then gradually add harder intervals from the enabled pool. Sight Reading does this with levels —
  interval should too.

  6. No "compare" after a wrong answer
  After a wrong answer you replay the original. But what the learner really needs is: "You heard this — now hear what you said — can you hear the difference?" A brief A/B button ("Hear your answer") after wrong would be the single most
  educational addition.

  ---
  Chords

  1. All 10 chord buttons always visible regardless of active group
  This is the biggest UX problem. When Group 1 (Major/Minor) is selected, all 10 buttons show — but 8 of them can never be correct. This makes the layout feel overwhelming and teaches the player to eliminate answers by exclusion rather
  than listening. Only show buttons for chords in the active group.

  2. Inversion feedback is invisible
  When inversions are enabled the player hears a 1st or 2nd inversion chord, answers correctly, and gets no information about what they actually heard. The feedback should say: "Correct! C Major — 1st Inversion." Over time this builds
  real recognition of voicings.

  3. Adaptive group progression punishes a single mistake too hard
  The adaptive mode (Group 4) uses raw streak to decide which chords appear. One wrong answer resets streak to 0, sending you back to Group 1 territory. This is frustrating. Use a rolling accuracy window (last 5 questions) instead of
  streak count for adaptive difficulty.

  4. No per-chord accuracy summary after the session
  The result screen shows total score. It should also show a breakdown: "Major: 4/4 ✓  Minor: 3/4  Dom7: 1/3 ✗" — this tells the player exactly what needs more work and makes the session feel meaningful.

  5. Chord group names mean nothing to a beginner
  "Group 2: Aug/Dim" — why is that harder? Why does it come after Group 1? A short descriptor on the group button would help: "Aug/Dim — unstable sounds", "7th chords — jazz flavour".

  ---
  Cadence

  1. Key is always C — answer becomes predictable fast
  _cadence_key exists in the code but is never randomized or user-configurable in the UI. After a few sessions the player starts recognizing the specific chord sounds in C rather than the cadence pattern. Even rotating through 3–4 keys
  (C, G, F, D) would break this. The setting just needs to be exposed or auto-rotated.

  2. Only 4 cadence types makes sessions feel thin
  With just 4 options, even a 10-question session becomes repetitive fast. Two quick additions that are musically important: Imperfect Authentic (V → I, but not in root position) and the Interrupted/Deceptive variant (V → IV). This would
   give 6 types and much more variety.

  3. No onboarding / context for what a "cadence" even is
  Interval and Chord modes at least have obvious labels. "Cadence" is a technical term — a new player has no idea what a "Perfect (V–I)" means. This mode needs a first-run intro overlay (same pattern as the ear training intro overlay
  already in the code) that plays each cadence type with its label before the quiz starts.

  4. No visual of the cadence structure
  When the two chords play, there's nothing on screen that shows "you're hearing chord I, then chord V". Even a simple Roman numeral display during playback (I → V → ?) that fills in the blanks when the answer is revealed would help
  learners build theory understanding alongside ear training.

  5. Broken chords toggle is buried
  This is genuinely one of the most effective ways to train — hearing chords as arpeggios instead of blocks is a completely different skill. It should be a prominent two-state button in the main session UI, not hidden in settings.
